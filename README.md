# Spintly Strategic Proposal — Product & Market Expansion

> A strategic product proposal for Spintly built as an interactive experience — not a slide deck.

**Live:** [spintly-portfolio.vercel.app](https://spintly-portfolio.vercel.app)

---

## Context

Spintly is a wireless access control platform deployed across enterprise office parks, commercial buildings, and gated communities. I worked directly with Spintly across 15+ enterprise parks — including buildings managing 100M+ sq ft and 80K+ active users — which gave me ground-level visibility into real operational pain points, user behavior, and product gaps that aren't visible from the outside.

This proposal was built for Spintly's product and leadership team to demonstrate:
1. Where the platform's current gaps are creating friction
2. What AI and automation opportunities exist within the access control workflow
3. How Spintly could expand from hardware-adjacent tooling into a broader workplace intelligence layer

---

## What's Inside

The interactive site is structured as a narrative strategy proposal:

- **Market Landscape** — Where Spintly sits relative to competitors (Openpath, Brivo, Genetec) and what the enterprise access control TAM looks like
- **Operational Pain Points** — Real issues observed across deployments: manual visitor management, app fatigue, disconnected facility workflows
- **AI Opportunities** — Specific AI-native features: anomaly detection in access logs, predictive access provisioning, automated visitor approval
- **Product Expansion Areas** — How Spintly could extend into space utilization, workplace analytics, and multi-site intelligence
- **Prioritization** — RICE-scored product bets with rationale
- **Go-to-Market** — Recommended segments and expansion strategy

---

## Why Interactive, Not a Deck

A slide deck forces linear consumption and hides the depth of research. An interactive site lets the reader navigate to what's most relevant — leadership can jump to market sizing, a PM can dig into feature prioritization, an engineer can look at system architecture implications.

This format also demonstrates that product thinking can be communicated without being buried in bullets.
